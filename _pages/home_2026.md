<section class="content-section about-section" id="about">
  <div class="section-heading">
    <h2>About</h2>
  </div>
  <div class="about-card">
    <p class="about-intro">
This winter school focuses on LLM-based software development, combining lectures, hands-on labs, and project-based learning. It introduces how large language models can be applied to software design, automated code generation, testing, usability, security, and privacy. Participants will gain practical skills and conceptual understanding to effectively integrate advanced AI techniques into modern software engineering workflows in both academic and industrial contexts.
    </p>
    <div class="about-details">
      <p>The winter school is primarily intended for PhD students, postdoctoral researchers, early-career academic researchers, and industry practitioners working in AI, software engineering, software interaction, and related fields.</p>
      <p>It will showcase advanced academic research and cutting-edge industry practices. Participants are therefore expected to have relevant experience or a background in LLM-based software development.</p>

    <p class="speaker-dialog-eyebrow">Talk by Andrea Stocco</p>
    <section class="speaker-dialog-section speaker-dialog-title-section">
      <h3>Title</h3>
      <h2 id="andrea-talk-title">Beyond Benchmarks: Systematic Testing of LLM-Based Software Systems</h2>
    </section>
    <section class="speaker-dialog-section">
      <h3>Abstract</h3>
      <p>Large language models are increasingly becoming integral components of software systems, yet their evaluation still largely relies on predefined benchmarks and collections of manually constructed inputs. Software testing poses a different question: rather than measuring performance on inputs we already have, how can we systematically explore the enormous space of possible interactions to uncover failures we do not yet know about? In this talk, I will discuss recent approaches for systematically testing LLM-based software systems. I will show how techniques from automated and search-based software testing can be used to explore large natural-language input spaces, guide test generation toward failures, and diversify generated tests to expose different system behaviors. I will exemplify these challenges using conversational systems, where failures may emerge only through sequences of interactions. Drawing on lessons from testing other AI-enabled systems, I will conclude with open challenges around test adequacy, failure oracles, and reproducibility, and discuss how these testing principles could extend to agentic systems.</p>
    </section>
    <section class="speaker-dialog-section">
      <h3>Bio</h3>
      <p>Andrea Stocco is an Assistant Professor at the Technical University of Munich (TUM) and Head of the Automated Software Testing field of competence at fortiss. His research focuses on automated software testing, with particular emphasis on testing AI-enabled systems, including deep learning models, autonomous driving systems, and LLM-based software. His work investigates techniques for systematic test generation, search-based testing, and the validation of AI systems across simulation and real-world environments. His research has been published at major software engineering venues including ICSE, FSE, ASE, and in journals such as IEEE TSE and ACM TOSEM.</p>

    <section class="speaker-dialog-section speaker-dialog-title-section">
      <h3>Title</h3>
      <h2 id="zhenchang-talk-title">AI-Native Requirements Engineering: Vision, Principles, and Early Prototype</h2>
    </section>
    <section class="speaker-dialog-section">
      <h3>Abstract</h3>
      <p>Large language models (LLMs) promise software from a single sentence, but that fluency solves generation, not the harder problem of knowing what to build and when to trust the result. This talk reframes requirements engineering as a second-order control problem: LLMs supply cognitive “energy,” yet compound uncertainty across humans, AI, tools, and organizations. I will present design principles that make unknowns first-class, as well as the conceptualization of requirements space, cognitive-state maps, fog ledgers, and space-aware AI for requirements engineering. Early prototypes will also be presented, showing how human and AI collaboratively drive the exploration, convergence, and validation of requirements.       <h3>Abstract</h3>
      <p>Manual debugging and repair of test failures during continuous integration checks is time-consuming and disruptive to developer flow. In this talk, we introduce FlowAgent, an autonomous AI coding agent deployed across Google to repair pre-submit test failures in continuous integration.       <h2 id="patrick-talk-title">Secure LLM-Assisted Software Engineering: From Dockerfiles to CIS Hardening</h2>
    </section>
    <section class="speaker-dialog-section">
      <h3>Abstract</h3>
      <p>Large language models are becoming part of everyday software engineering, but their security value and limitations need to be assessed in the context of real industrial workflows. This talk presents three perspectives from Siemens research and practice. First, we examine how LLMs can support the creation of more secure Dockerfiles in our ASE industry showcase paper. We then discuss the security challenges of deploying LLM-assisted software engineering in an enterprise: dependency confusion, prompt injection, and the potential leakage of sensitive source code, configuration, or business data. Finally, we use automatic configuration hardening of new Linux-based systems against Center for Internet Security (CIS) recommendations as a concrete example of a task that current LLM-based assistance does not yet perform reliably. The example is intended to spark discussion about why plausible advice is not the same as complete, verifiable, and safe automation, and where the limitations of today's LLMs become decisive.</p>

          bio.hidden = view === "bio" && speakerName && heading && heading.textContent.trim() !== speakerName;

          dialog.removeAttribute("aria-labelledby");

        document.body.classList.remove("speaker-dialog-open");

        var bounds = dialog.getBoundingClientRect();
        var outsideDialog = event.clientX < bounds.left || event.clientX > bounds.right || event.clientY < bounds.top || event.clientY > bounds.bottom;

        if (outsideDialog) {
